The gatekeeper. It checks the signature of every update, and it only knows today's algorithm.

New firmware

ML-DSA signed

Post-quantum signature. Built, signed and booted fine in the lab.

Why now

Signatures like ECDSA are expected to fall to future quantum computers. NIST standardised ML-DSA in 2024, and firmware signing is starting to move.[1]

Who feels it

Industrial IoT makers, e.g. wireless condition-monitoring sensors, often installed where they are hard to reach. Our demo chip is used in such sensors.[2]

What a missing bridge costs

If the gatekeeper rejects the update and can't replace itself, the fix is manual: a technician, a cable, a visit, device by device.

Most tests check the destination. ReleaseProof tests the bridge.

Backup · what is real, what is not

Scoped engineering evidence, stated honestly

Real today

  • Upstream bootloader code at pinned commits, compiled with pinned toolchains on every run
  • Ephemeral signing keys; real binaries booted and updated in Renode
  • Negative tests: tampered image, wrong key, old bootloader receiving the new image
  • Deterministic V1.2 engine with golden-hash regression tests
  • Re-evaluations as new runs with a parent reference; history immutable
  • Failure handling: runner killed ⇒ FAILED, no verdict, nothing replayed
  • Evidence pack download and strict preflight CLI with engine replay

Not claimed

  • Results on physical silicon: this is emulation
  • Power-loss / interrupted-update safety: stays UNKNOWN (INV-I4)
  • Support for arbitrary or proprietary firmware: 2 configurations only
  • Certification or regulatory compliance: non-official assessment
  • Semantic truth of every AI sentence: checks cover IDs and verdict labels
  • Managed GPU identity, benchmark numbers, customers or ROI
  • Hosted CI execution or OTA integration
